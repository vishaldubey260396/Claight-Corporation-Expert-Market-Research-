Importantly, Body Mass Index alone does not fully capture individual health risk: it cannot distinguish fat mass from lean muscle mass, nor does it characterise fat distribution, meaning two patients with an identical BMI can carry materially different clinical risk profiles.

Causes

Obesity arises from a sustained imbalance between energy intake and expenditure, but the underlying drivers of that imbalance are heterogeneous and frequently overlapping across four broad categories.

  • Genetic and Monogenic Factors: Polygenic susceptibility affects appetite and metabolism, while rare monogenic forms (LEP, LEPR, MC4R, POMC, PCSK1 mutations) cause severe early-onset obesity; MC4R deficiency alone accounts for up to 6% of such cases.
  • Environmental and Behavioural Factors: Obesogenic food environments, sedentary behaviour, chronic sleep deprivation and psychosocial stress collectively drive sustained positive energy balance.
  • Endocrine and Metabolic Causes: Hypothyroidism, Cushing's syndrome, PCOS and, less commonly, hypothalamic injury or tumours can each drive secondary obesity.
  • Pharmacological Causes: Certain antipsychotics, corticosteroids, antidepressants, anticonvulsants and diabetes medications carry a well-documented weight-gain side-effect profile.

The combination of genetic and hormonal predisposition with an increasingly obesogenic environment helps explain why global obesity prevalence has risen far faster over the past four decades than any underlying shift in population genetics could account for alone.

Because BMI-based classification does not capture individual variation in health risk at a given body size, the Edmonton Obesity Staging System (EOSS) has emerged as a widely used clinical staging framework that grades disease severity based on medical, functional and psychological impact rather than body size alone.

Stages of Obesity

Obesity follows a progressive clinical trajectory rather than representing a fixed state. In a large population cohort applying the Edmonton Obesity Staging System, only 0.4% of individuals with obesity fell into Stage 0, while 42.3% and 48.9% were classified into Stage 2 and Stage 3 respectively, underscoring how rarely obesity presents without measurable health impact. Pathophysiology

Adipose tissue is now understood as a metabolically active endocrine organ rather than a passive energy store. In obesity, adipocyte hypertrophy and hyperplasia are accompanied by a shift in the tissue's secretory profile, termed adipokines, including leptin, adiponectin, resistin and visfatin, alongside pro-inflammatory cytokines such as tumour necrosis factor-alpha, interleukin-6 and monocyte chemoattractant protein-1. This shift drives a state of chronic, low-grade systemic inflammation that is now considered central to obesity's downstream metabolic consequences.

A key mechanism is leptin resistance: although circulating leptin rises in proportion to fat mass, hypothalamic signalling becomes progressively less responsive to it, driven in part by endoplasmic reticulum stress and upregulation of suppressor proteins such as SOCS3, blunting leptin's normal appetite-suppressing, energy-expenditure-promoting effect and perpetuating further weight gain. In parallel, excess free fatty acid release and adipose tissue macrophage infiltration contribute directly to insulin resistance, the mechanistic link between obesity and type 2 diabetes. Emerging research also implicates gut microbiome composition and the gut-brain axis in appetite regulation and energy harvest from diet, an active area of ongoing mechanistic and therapeutic investigation.

Screening and Diagnosis

  • Step 1: Calculate Body Mass Index (weight in kilograms divided by height in metres squared) as the initial population-level screening measure.
  • Step 2: Measure waist circumference to assess central adiposity, since BMI alone does not distinguish fat from lean mass or characterise fat distribution.
  • Step 3: Screen for cardiometabolic comorbidities via blood pressure measurement, a fasting lipid panel, and fasting glucose or HbA1c.
  • Step 4: Where indicated, assess body composition directly via DEXA scanning or bioelectrical impedance analysis for a more precise measure than BMI alone.
  • Step 5: Evaluate for secondary or endocrine causes, including thyroid function and cortisol, in patients with atypical presentation or treatment-resistant weight gain.
  • Step 6: Apply a clinical staging framework, such as the Edmonton Obesity Staging System, to characterise disease severity beyond body size alone.
  • Step 7: Assess eligibility for pharmacotherapy or bariatric surgery against established BMI-plus-comorbidity thresholds: typically a BMI of 30 or above, or 27 or above with a qualifying comorbidity, for pharmacotherapy, and a BMI of 35 with comorbidity or 40 alone for surgical intervention.

Global Disease Burden and Socioeconomic Context

Obesity represents one of the most consequential chronic disease burdens globally, both in direct clinical terms and in its downstream economic footprint. Global economic costs attributable to overweight and obesity are projected to exceed USD 4.32 trillion annually by 2035, equivalent to roughly 3% of global GDP, encompassing direct healthcare expenditure alongside the substantial indirect costs of lost productivity, absenteeism and premature mortality. Obesity-related comorbidities, particularly type 2 diabetes, cardiovascular disease and several cancers, collectively contribute to more than 5 million deaths annually worldwide.

The burden is increasingly concentrated in low- and middle-income countries: over 70% of individuals living with obesity now reside in developing nations, reflecting the rapid nutritional and lifestyle transition accompanying urbanisation, even as these health systems often remain least equipped to deliver comprehensive obesity care. This inequity extends to diagnosis and treatment access, with patients in lower-resource settings considerably less likely to receive body-composition assessment, specialist referral, or access to newer pharmacotherapy, reinforcing existing health disparities.

Stigma remains a further, often underappreciated dimension of obesity's socioeconomic burden. Weight-based stigma in healthcare, workplace and social settings is well documented and is independently associated with reduced healthcare-seeking behaviour, poorer mental health outcomes and, paradoxically, further weight gain through stress-related physiological and behavioural pathways, underscoring that addressing obesity's societal burden requires more than clinical or pharmacological intervention alone.

Epidemiology and Disease Burden

Obesity is now recognised by public-health authorities as one of the most significant chronic-disease challenges facing the world. According to the World Health Organization, approximately 16% of the world's adults were living with obesity in 2022, and the global prevalence of adult obesity has more than doubled since 1990. In absolute terms, more than 1 billion people worldwide are now living with obesity. A further 2.5 billion adults, or 43% of the global adult population, were classified as overweight in 2022, up from 25% in 1990.

Comorbidity and Economic Burden

Obesity rarely presents in isolation; it is a principal upstream driver of the broader chronic-disease burden that dominates healthcare spending in every major market covered in this report. The World Health Organization identifies obesity as a major risk factor for type 2 diabetes, with obese individuals facing a materially elevated risk of developing the condition relative to individuals of normal weight, and as a contributing factor in an estimated 20-30% of cardiovascular disease deaths globally. This comorbidity profile is central to the commercial logic now driving pharmaceutical label-expansion strategy: Novo Nordisk's SELECT trial, demonstrating a 20% reduction in the risk of heart attack, stroke or cardiovascular death among overweight patients with semaglutide, and similar cardiometabolic and MASH-focused development programmes from Boehringer Ingelheim, Roche and others, are each designed to reposition obesity therapy as cardiovascular and metabolic-disease prevention, a framing that materially strengthens the case for insurance reimbursement.

From a health-economic perspective, the direct medical costs associated with treating obesity-related comorbidities, spanning diabetes management, cardiovascular intervention, joint replacement surgery and cancer care, are estimated by public-health bodies to run into the hundreds of billions of dollars annually across major economies, a burden that materially strengthens the argument payers and governments are increasingly willing to entertain: that upfront investment in effective obesity pharmacotherapy or surgery may be offset, over a sufficiently long time horizon, by avoided downstream comorbidity costs. This is precisely the cost-effectiveness argument underpinning France's 2026 reimbursement decision and the rationale several other European governments are understood to be evaluating as they consider following France's lead.

Clinical Trial Activity

Clinical trial volumes in the obesity therapeutic area have risen sharply over the past three years, mirroring the scale of capital being deployed into the category. Trial activity is concentrated in Phase II and Phase III studies of incretin-based mechanisms, with an increasing share of studies now focused on cardiometabolic and other label-expanding indications, including cardiovascular risk reduction, obstructive sleep apnoea, knee osteoarthritis and metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH), reflecting an industry-wide strategy of maximising the lifetime value of each molecule across multiple indications rather than obesity alone.

Trial geography has also broadened considerably: whereas early GLP-1 obesity trials enrolled overwhelmingly from North America and Western Europe, current Phase III programmes increasingly include dedicated cohorts from China, Japan and other Asian markets, both to satisfy local regulatory requirements for approval and to generate the ethnicity-specific efficacy and dosing data that regulators in these markets typically require before granting reimbursement. Trial design is also evolving beyond simple weight-loss endpoints toward composite cardiometabolic and quality-of-life measures, reflecting both scientific maturation of the field and a deliberate commercial strategy of building the broadest possible evidentiary base for future payer negotiations.

Pipeline by Mechanism of Action

  • Triple Agonists (GLP-1/GIP/Glucagon): Eli Lilly's retatrutide has delivered the highest weight reduction reported to date in a Phase III obesity trial, with the highest dose achieving a mean weight loss of 28.7% over 68 weeks, alongside meaningful improvement in osteoarthritis-related pain in its first successful Phase III readout.
  • Dual GLP-1/GIP Agonists: Eli Lilly's tirzepatide (Mounjaro/Zepbound) remains the category benchmark, delivering approximately 20% body-weight reduction in pivotal trials. Viking Therapeutics' VK2735 is being developed in both subcutaneous and oral formulations, with the Phase III VANQUISH-1 and VANQUISH-2 trials fully enrolled as of early 2026 and an oral Phase III trial expected to begin in Q4 2026. Roche's CT-388 (enicepatide), acquired through the 2024 Carmot Therapeutics deal, achieved 22.5% greater weight loss than placebo after 48 weeks in Phase II.
  • Contrarian GLP-1 Agonist / GIP Antagonist: Amgen's MariTide takes a differentiated mechanistic approach, activating the GLP-1 receptor while blocking the GIP receptor. In its Phase II study, adults without type 2 diabetes lost up to approximately 20% of body weight with once-monthly dosing, and the Phase III MARITIME programme is now evaluating the molecule across obesity, sleep apnoea and cardiovascular disease.
  • GLP-1/Glucagon Agonists: Boehringer Ingelheim and Zealand Pharma's survodutide, which combines GLP-1 and glucagon receptor agonism, achieved approximately 19% weight loss at 48 weeks in Phase II and is advancing through the four-trial Phase III SYNCHRONIZE programme, spanning obesity, type 2 diabetes and MASH, with first Phase III readouts expected in 2026.
  • Amylin Analogues: Novo Nordisk's amycretin (a unimolecular GLP-1/amylin agonist) demonstrated 14.5% weight loss over 36 weeks in Phase II and is entering Phase III in 2026; the company's CagriSema (semaglutide plus cagrilintide) achieved 22.7% weight loss at 68 weeks in the REDEFINE 1 trial, with an FDA filing expected in late 2026, although the separate REDEFINE 4 trial narrowly missed its primary endpoint despite 23% weight loss. Zealand Pharma and Roche's petrelintide, a once-weekly amylin analogue, is scheduled to enter Phase III for chronic weight management in H2 2026.
  • Oral Small-Molecule GLP-1 Agonists: Eli Lilly's orforglipron (branded Foundayo), an oral non-peptide GLP-1 agonist with no food or water restrictions, achieved an 11.2% mean weight loss over 72 weeks in the Phase III ATTAIN-1 trial and has since secured FDA approval. Novo Nordisk's oral semaglutide 25mg formulation (the Wegovy tablet) achieved a 13.6% mean weight loss over 64 weeks in the OASIS-4 trial and launched in the United States in January 2026, booking USD 2.26 billion in its first quarter on the market with over one million patients initiated.
  • Emerging and Regional Entrants: Pfizer re-entered the obesity race through its acquisition of Metsera, gaining PF-08653944, an ultra-long-acting injectable GLP-1 candidate with positive Phase IIb data and ten Phase III trials expected to advance in 2026. In China, Innovent Biologics' mazdutide (Xinermei) launched in 2025 and Sciwind Biosciences' ecnoglutide launched in 2026, while Kailera Therapeutics is advancing ribupatide in partnership with Hengrui Pharma through a global Phase III programme.

Obesity Market Dynamics

Market Opportunities

Beyond the structural drivers already sustaining category growth, several distinct, less fully exploited avenues offer additional upside for manufacturers and investors over the forecast period.

  • 1. Under-Penetrated Emerging Markets: India's estimated 150 million individuals living with obesity, alongside similarly under-penetrated patient pools across Latin America and parts of Asia Pacific, represent a substantial addressable population that current treatment rates have barely begun to reach.
  • 2. Oral Formulation Expansion: The scaling of oral GLP-1 formulations, including Novo Nordisk's oral Wegovy tablet and Eli Lilly's orforglipron, removes the injection barrier that has historically constrained uptake among needle-averse patients and enables broader prescribing in primary-care settings.
  • 3. Next-Generation Multi-Receptor and Combination Therapies: Next-generation multi-receptor agonists such as retatrutide, together with combination regimens pairing incretin therapy with muscle-preserving or metabolic adjuncts, offer a premium efficacy tier and a fresh commercial growth cycle.
  • 4. Label Expansion into Adjacent Indications: Continued label expansion into cardiovascular risk reduction, obstructive sleep apnoea, MASH and other comorbidities widens the reimbursement and prescribing rationale for GLP-1 therapy well beyond weight loss alone.

Market Drivers and Constraints

Efficacy gains and disease burden outweigh cost and access frictions over the forecast horizon.

Market Drivers

  • 1. Rising Prevalence of Obesity: The single most important structural driver of this market is the sheer scale and continued growth of the underlying disease population. With over 1 billion people living with obesity globally and prevalence still climbing in both developed and emerging markets, the addressable patient population for treatment continues to expand faster than most other chronic disease categories.
  • 2. GLP-1 and Incretin Efficacy Breakthrough: The clinical performance of modern incretin therapies, delivering 15-28% body-weight reduction, has transformed obesity from a condition managed primarily through lifestyle counselling and surgery into one increasingly addressed through chronic pharmacological management, comparable in ambition to how statins transformed the management of hyperlipidaemia.
  • 3. Expanding Reimbursement and Access: The United States' extension of Medicare coverage to obesity GLP-1 therapy from July 1, 2026, and France's landmark reimbursement of Wegovy and Mounjaro from June 15, 2026 at 65-100% depending on comorbidity status, are expected to be followed by further coverage expansion across other European markets.
  • 4. Digital Health and Direct-to-Consumer Models: Digital health platforms integrating telehealth, pharmacy fulfilment and adherence support, such as Eli Lilly's LillyDirect, are lowering the friction associated with initiating and continuing obesity therapy, while employer-sponsored coverage programmes are emerging as an important channel for patient access.

Market Constraints

  • 1. High Treatment Cost and Pricing Pressure: List prices for branded GLP-1 therapies remain high in unreimbursed markets, at approximately USD 1,350 per month in the United States, restricting access for cash-paying patients and inviting political intervention, including Most Favoured Nation pricing pressure and Novo Nordisk's planned 50% Wegovy list-price cut from January 2027.
  • 2. Low Long-Term Treatment Adherence: Persistence on GLP-1 therapy beyond the initial treatment period remains a challenge in real-world settings; industry claims data indicates only a small proportion of patients remain on therapy after three years, and patients who discontinue treatment regain weight considerably faster than those who discontinue conventional diet and exercise programmes.
  • 3. Regulatory Crackdown on Compounding: Compounded, non-FDA-approved versions of semaglutide and tirzepatide expanded rapidly during the 2022-2024 branded-drug shortage period. With both molecules removed from the FDA's official shortage list by early 2025 and the agency proposing in April 2026 to permanently exclude them, along with liraglutide, from the compounding list, this lower-cost access channel is being progressively closed.
  • 4. Side-Effect and Tolerability Concerns: Gastrointestinal side effects remain the most commonly cited reason for treatment discontinuation, and the injection burden associated with parenteral formulations continues to be a barrier for needle-averse patients, a factor expected to ease somewhat as oral formulations scale.

Key Price Indicators

Pricing for GLP-1-class obesity therapy is shaped by a distinct set of structural factors that vary meaningfully across the markets covered in this report, more than by manufacturing cost alone.

  • Reimbursement and Payer Classification: Whether a national health system classifies obesity treatment as a reimbursable chronic-disease therapy or excludes it as elective or lifestyle medicine is the single largest determinant of the price a patient ultimately faces.
  • Government Negotiating Leverage: Single-payer and centralised-formulary systems command materially stronger price-negotiation leverage with manufacturers than fragmented, multi-payer commercial insurance markets such as the United States.
  • Patent Exclusivity and Competitive Intensity: Pricing power remains highest while a molecule retains patent protection and faces limited direct competition; the arrival of oral formulations and, later in the decade, biosimilar and generic entry is expected to exert sustained downward pressure.
  • Manufacturing and Supply-Chain Cost Recovery: Fill-finish capacity constraints and the complexity of peptide manufacturing have historically supported premium pricing, though continued capacity expansion by Novo Nordisk, Eli Lilly and contract manufacturers is gradually easing this constraint.
  • Cross-Border Reference Pricing: Many European markets explicitly benchmark negotiated prices against a basket of peer countries, meaning a landmark reimbursement decision in one market, such as France's 2026 decision, can anchor negotiating positions across the region.
  • Currency and Macroeconomic Conditions: List prices set in US dollars can translate into materially different local out-of-pocket costs as exchange rates and local purchasing power vary across the markets covered in this report.

Europe

Europe's obesity market is undergoing a pivotal reimbursement transition. The French Health Ministry estimated the annual cost to public finances at approximately EUR 100 million at full rollout.

In the United Kingdom, the NHS covers Wegovy and Mounjaro through specialist weight-management pathways, while a private prescription market also exists at GBP 199-299 per four-week supply; Eli Lilly's roughly 170% list-price increase on Mounjaro for out-of-pocket purchasers in September 2025 pushed the UK obesity drug market past GBP 2 billion in annual value by year-end 2025. Germany, Europe's largest pharmaceutical market by value, presents a more restrictive picture: statutory health insurance classifies obesity treatment as a "lifestyle medicine" and excludes GLP-1 therapy from standard reimbursement, leaving patients to pay approximately EUR 250-350 per month out of pocket. Italy and Spain are expected to follow France's lead in reimbursement policy over the medium term, albeit at a more measured pace.

Asia Pacific

Japan represents a mature, ageing-population market in which obesity prevalence, while lower than in Western markets, is compounded by a very high burden of obesity-related type 2 diabetes and cardiometabolic disease. India represents one of the largest under-penetrated patient populations covered in this report, with an estimated 150 million individuals living with obesity, yet comparatively low current treatment penetration. India's large domestic generics and biosimilar manufacturing base positions the country to become an early and significant market for lower-cost follow-on GLP-1 products once core patents begin to lapse. China stands out as an increasingly important growth market and a source of innovation in its own right: Innovent Biologics and Sciwind Biosciences have each launched domestically developed GLP-1 obesity therapies (mazdutide and ecnoglutide respectively), while Hengrui Pharmaceutical, Hansoh Pharmaceutical and Kailera Therapeutics are advancing their own late-stage candidates.

South America

South America represents a comparatively small but steadily growing market, led by Brazil, the region's largest economy and pharmaceutical market, followed by Argentina and Colombia. Private insurance and out-of-pocket payment remain the primary channels through which patients access GLP-1 obesity therapy across the region, given the limited extent of public reimbursement for weight-management indications. Brazil's large private hospital and pharmacy network, alongside a sizeable and growing middle-class patient base, positions the country as the natural anchor market for regional obesity-therapy expansion. Affordability and currency volatility remain the principal near-term constraints on faster regional growth.

For strategic planning purposes, patent-cliff timing represents one of the single most consequential variables shaping the long-run structure of this market: the sequencing of core-molecule patent expiries relative to the maturation of oral and next-generation multi-receptor franchises will determine how much of the current duopoly's economic rent can be defended into the next decade, and how quickly price competition and broader access follow.

Regulatory Framework

Regulatory oversight of the obesity market operates at two distinct levels: market-authorisation agencies that determine whether a therapy or device may be sold at all, and, in most markets outside the United States, a separate health-technology-assessment and pricing authority that determines whether, and at what price, that therapy will be reimbursed by public or statutory insurance. This report's regional analysis illustrates how material a factor the second layer, reimbursement policy, has become in determining actual patient access, often mattering more than the underlying speed of clinical approval itself.

  • United States (FDA): The FDA regulates the approval of obesity pharmacotherapy and medical devices, and has approved a growing roster of GLP-1 and dual/triple-agonist therapies for chronic weight management since 2021. Following the resolution of branded-supply shortages in 2024-2025, the agency has shifted its regulatory focus toward closing the compounding pathway, with a further proposal in April 2026 to permanently exclude semaglutide, tirzepatide and liraglutide from the substances eligible for large-scale compounding.
  • Europe (EMA and National HTA Bodies): The European Medicines Agency (EMA) and national health technology assessment bodies, such as France's Haute Autorite de Sante (HAS) and the UK's National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE), govern both market authorisation and the reimbursement pathway, with national payers such as France's Comite Economique des Produits de Sante (CEPS) responsible for price negotiation.
  • Japan (PMDA): The Pharmaceuticals and Medical Devices Agency (PMDA) governs approval, working alongside the national health insurance system's drug-pricing organisation to set reimbursed prices, typically at a discount to U.S. and, increasingly, European list prices.
  • India (CDSCO): The Central Drugs Standard Control Organisation (CDSCO) oversees market authorisation; given the near-total absence of public reimbursement for obesity pharmacotherapy, affordability and out-of-pocket pricing, rather than regulatory approval timelines, are the principal gating factors for market expansion.

Across all markets, a common regulatory theme is the increasing use of label expansion, particularly cardiovascular risk reduction, as a lever to secure broader reimbursement for therapies initially approved on a weight-loss indication alone, alongside continued regulatory harmonisation across major markets as a key determinant of both market access and commercial trajectory over the forecast period.

Key Opinion Leader (KOL) Insights

This additional-insight section synthesises perspectives gathered through this study's primary research programme with practising endocrinologists, bariatric surgeons and obesity-medicine specialists across the five regions covered. Key opinion leaders consulted for this report were broadly consistent on several themes shaping clinical practice ahead of, and independent of, formal treatment guideline updates.

  • Obesity as Chronic Disease: Specialists increasingly frame obesity management as a lifelong, relapsing-condition model analogous to hypertension or type 2 diabetes, rather than a course of treatment with a defined endpoint, a shift with material implications for how payers should evaluate the long-run cost-effectiveness of GLP-1 therapy.
  • Sequencing of Therapy: A growing number of bariatric surgeons report using GLP-1 therapy pre-operatively to reduce surgical risk and post-operatively to consolidate weight loss and manage weight regain, reinforcing this report's view that pharmacotherapy and surgery are becoming complementary rather than purely substitutive pathways.
  • Muscle Mass and Body Composition: Clinicians consulted flagged preservation of lean muscle mass during rapid pharmacological weight loss as an emerging area of clinical concern and commercial opportunity, likely to shape the design of next-generation combination therapies and adjunctive resistance-training and nutritional protocols.
  • Access as the Binding Constraint: Across nearly every market covered in this study, specialists identified reimbursement and out-of-pocket cost, rather than drug supply or clinical evidence, as the primary barrier standing between current prescribing practice and the level of treatment that underlying epidemiological need would otherwise support.

Conclusion and Outlook

The global obesity market stands at an inflection point. What was, for decades, a market anchored around bariatric surgery and modestly effective pharmacotherapy has been fundamentally reshaped by the clinical breakthrough of GLP-1 and multi-receptor incretin therapies, which have driven the market from an estimated USD 8.5 billion in 2019 to USD 18.61 billion in 2025 and are projected to carry it to approximately USD 48.71 billion by 2035. The forward decade of growth will be defined less by further efficacy breakthroughs (though retatrutide and CagriSema suggest more may be coming) and more by the pace at which access barriers are dismantled: the transition to oral formulations, the expansion of reimbursement across Medicare and major European markets, and the resolution of manufacturing capacity constraints that have, for much of this decade, throttled supply well below underlying epidemiological demand.

For manufacturers, the central strategic question is no longer whether to compete in obesity, but how: through internal development, as Eli Lilly and Novo Nordisk have done; through acquisition, as Roche and Pfizer have chosen; or through geographically differentiated innovation, as a growing cohort of Chinese biopharmaceutical companies are pursuing. For payers and governments, the question is how quickly to extend coverage for a therapy class that is clinically transformative but fiscally significant at scale. For investors, the market offers rare visibility into a large, structurally underpenetrated therapeutic category, tempered by real near-term risks around pricing policy, patent life and long-term adherence.

Looking specifically to the back half of the forecast period, three developments merit close monitoring: the timing and magnitude of the first true patent cliffs for semaglutide and tirzepatide and the resulting pace of biosimilar or generic entry; the extent to which oral, small-molecule incretin therapies can approach the efficacy of injectable formulations at meaningfully lower manufacturing cost, thereby expanding affordability in price-sensitive markets such as India and much of Latin America; and whether label expansion into cardiovascular and other comorbidity indications succeeds in permanently repositioning obesity pharmacotherapy within mainstream cardiometabolic disease budgets, rather than being treated as a discretionary lifestyle benefit subject to periodic reimbursement rollback.
